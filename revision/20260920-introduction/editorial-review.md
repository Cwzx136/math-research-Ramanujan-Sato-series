## Findings

- The opening clearly states the problem and separates constructing CM identities from proving a converse. It identifies the three classification tasks—uniqueness, CM forcing, and arithmetic exhaustion—before introducing notation.
- Prior work receives a coherent, selective discussion: modular constructions and the established 36 standard evaluations; extensions to other coefficients and geometries; and the transcendence/comparison results relevant to the converse. The text does not present the known evaluations as new or imply that earlier CM constructions settle non-CM exhaustion.
- The exact problem is defined before the principal results. The chosen power-series branch, ordinary convergence, primitive coefficient-pair normalization, sign convention, and initially permitted zero multiplier are explicit. The explanation of why primitivity must concern the pair also addresses the ambiguity in the initial research question.
- The core ideas are explained through one organizing object, the de Rham identity line. The progression from Clausen and Gauss–Manin to uniqueness, the complementary CM eigenline, arithmetic descent, and the comparison obstruction is conceptually unified and avoids polynomial accumulation.
- The arithmetic criteria and scarcity results are summarized with their essential restrictions: density greater than 3/4, exact ordinary Frobenius preservation, bounded degree of the squared multiplier, and complete arithmetic height windows. They are expressly distinguished from deductions from the scalar identity alone.
- The organization paragraph corresponds to the actual input order in the master and explains the role of the appendices without reproducing their entire contents.
- The prose matches the restrained mathematical tone of the Hint introduction. The longer treatment is justified by the present paper's additional arithmetic and scarcity results. Some themes recur in the opening, theorem statements, and conceptual discussion, but those passages serve distinct functions and do not require deletion.
- The conditional classification remains the **first principal theorem**. Only the definition and the explicit quadratic-period hypothesis precede it; unconditional uniqueness follows it. The title, abstract, introduction, and closing assertion consistently leave unrestricted CM forcing unresolved.
