\begin{abstract}
A first-order identity for an elliptic period square determines a line
in de Rham cohomology. Chudnovsky's theorem makes this line unique;
complex multiplication supplies it as the eigenline complementary to
the Hodge filtration. We use this interpretation to classify the CM
identities in the four standard Ramanujan--Sato families: there are
36 primitive standard classes and 35 linear Euler companions.
Exhaustion outside the CM locus follows from an explicitly stated
quadratic-period independence hypothesis. We express that hypothesis
in modular jets and distinguish it from the established theorems on
complete comparison tensors.

Two unconditional arithmetic criteria complement this complex theory.
Weighted congruences at lower prime density greater than $3/4$
characterize the standard classes, while exact preservation of the
Hodge line by crystalline Frobenius at one ordinary place characterizes
CM. Variation of the periods also gives divergent denominator gaps for
bounded multiplier degree and subpower counts in bounded height
windows. The proofs incorporate the precise specialization,
algebraization, and counting theorems on which these conclusions
depend. Auxiliary constructions, coordinate certificates, further
families, and countertests are developed in the appendices.
The implication from a single complex identity to CM remains unproved.
\end{abstract}

\section*{The remaining assertion}
The complex identity fixes one value of a quadratic period pairing.
At a CM fiber this value, the complementary eigenline, and the
arithmetic congruences agree. The converse would require deriving
the extra comparison or Frobenius information from that single value.
Neither the complete-tensor theorems nor the scarcity theorems provide
this step. Accordingly, existence of the tabulated identities and
projective uniqueness are unconditional; their unrestricted exhaustion
remains conditional on Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:QPI}.
